Why it matters

These are the reasons we care. Some are facts and some are our worries, so we have kept them apart. Our opinions are in the pink boxes.

The double blind bend

The Plough's car park opens onto Hundon Road at a double blind bend, close to where Buntry Lane and Simms Lane join it.

On 27 June 2026 the car park filled for a wedding reception, and cars were parked along the entrance and on the verge. The owners' brochure says the site can take "large vehicles including coaches".

In 2014 the council at the time, St Edmundsbury Borough Council, refused an application to use garden land at Hundon Grange for a wedding marquee. Hundon Grange is about 0.7 miles along the same road. The County Highway Authority recommended refusal on highway safety grounds, saying the access had "substandard visibility".

Sources: the campaign organiser's photos, 27 June 2026. The owners' brochure, April 2027 to December 2028. Planning application DC/14/0144/FUL on the West Suffolk planning register, refused 24 March 2014.

Our view

Events bring a lot of cars arriving and leaving within a short time, and some could leave late at night. On a double blind bend we think that is a real risk. The highway authority objected to wedding traffic at Hundon Grange, whose access is not on a double blind bend. We think the concern at The Plough is at least as great.

Late nights

The premises licence for The Plough allows alcohol sales until midnight from Sunday to Thursday, and until 1am on Fridays and Saturdays. The owners have set aside Fridays and Saturdays for private events.

Their brochure welcomes hen and stag groups and offers extras including karaoke hire, bouncy castles, an assault course and "It's a Knock Out" events.

Brockley Green is a small hamlet. Several homes are close to the building, including the house next door.

Sources: West Suffolk licence register, licence PL06372, checked 28 October 2026. The owners' opening times and brochure.

Our view

Private parties with music, outdoor games and possible late finishes are a different thing from a village pub. A planning application would let the council consider planning conditions on hours, numbers and noise. Because there has been no application, there are no planning conditions.

The walk to the pub

The roads out of Brockley Green have no footway and no street lights, and there is no bus. The nearest other pubs are the Barnardiston Arms in Kedington, about 1.7 miles away by road, and the Rose and Crown in Hundon, about 1.9 miles.

West Suffolk Council listed The Plough as an Asset of Community Value in July 2026. To list it, the council had to be satisfied that the pub had furthered the social wellbeing or interests of local people. It also had to think the pub could realistically do so again.

National planning policy, updated in August 2026, names pubs as key community facilities that should be kept unless strict tests are met.

Sources: road distances measured from The Plough's postcode. West Suffolk Council's list of assets of community value. National Planning Policy Framework (PDF), policy HC6.

Our view

For people here who do not drive, or who would rather not drive to the pub, losing The Plough means losing a pub altogether. That matters most to older residents and to anyone on their own.

Two venues on one road

Suffolk Retreats, at Hundon Grange, is about 0.7 miles along the same road. Its website gives the same two phone numbers as The Plough Country Lodge. It markets wedding reception packages, hen and stag groups and accommodation for up to 24 guests.

Source: Suffolk Retreats website, seen 28 October 2026.

Our view

Two event venues on the same road would add to each other's traffic and noise. The area would gain nothing it does not already have, and it would lose its pub.

A pub with a long history

The Plough was an inn from at least the 1850s. It was run by the same family from 1957, and it appeared in the Good Pub Guide from 1986. It hosted quiz nights, charity evenings, the 2022 Jubilee and meetings of local clubs.

Sources: the owners' website and the Asset of Community Value evidence, January 2026. See what has happened for more.

Our view

For a long time The Plough was where the hamlet met. That is worth looking after.
